# T4, two options for the sponsor (draft, NOT frozen, NOT witnessed)

*Drafted 2026-09-19 after batch 1. Nothing here is a rule until one option is
written into `DECLARATION-T4.md`, frozen with `scripts/freeze` and witnessed.
No correlation, split or figure below has been computed on any day; the
thresholds were set without looking at 1 to 15 September for this purpose.*

## Common to both options

- **Why T4 exists.** The page note says it: on the seed days with the most
  wind bids NESO's Constraints figure ran at about 130 % of the two cuts, and
  on 2 and 9 September at about half. That pattern was seen after the numbers
  were, so it can only be tested on days nobody has seen.
- **Window.** Tracked days with settlement date **on or after 2026-09-16**
  (batch 2 onward). No day from 1 to 15 September counts; they are the days
  that suggested the idea.
- **Deadline.** `tracker-013` fetches batch 2 at **09:00 UTC on 2026-09-26**.
  The option chosen must be frozen and witnessed before then, or its window
  starts at batch 3. NESO's cost files are pinned daily and synced to the
  laptop; nobody opens them for days after 2026-09-09 before the freeze. On
  2026-09-19 they end at 2026-09-09.
- **L1** is NESO's Constraints £ at its first vintage, as 013 already records
  it (`outcome.l1_constraints_gbp`); later revisions are listed, never used.
- **Decided once.** Verdict at the falsifier date, **2027-03-31**, on every
  qualifying day. Until then the page shows "undecided" and the count of
  qualifying days, with no running statistic, so nobody can stop early on a
  good run.
- **T2 stays failed.** T4 is a new proposition, not a repair of T2.

## Option A: the wind share (as asked, made sign-robust)

**T4-A.** Across qualifying days, NESO's Constraints figure as a share of the
two cuts rises with the wind share of accepted bid volume: Spearman's rank
correlation **ρ ≥ 0.50**.

- **x, the wind share.** Accepted bid MWh on units the register types `WIND`,
  divided by the day's accepted bid MWh from system prices:
  `wind_bid_mwh / reconciliation.settlement_mwh.bid` (both absolute).
  Existing tracker fields only; no new data.
- **Why not the £ wind-bid share of paid-out, as first worded.** Its
  numerator is the positive-rows reading of wind-unit bid cashflow, which is
  ambiguous whenever the sign check fails. It failed on 6 of 7 batch-1 days, so
  a proposition decided by it would be decided by a number with two readings.
  The volume share has no sign. The £ share is reported beside the result as
  context and does not decide.
- **y.** `outcome.ratio_l1_to_two_cut` (first vintage).
- **Qualifying day.** Settlement date ≥ 2026-09-16; row available; a DISPTAV
  type reconciles (volume check passed); L1 exists at its first vintage by the
  falsifier date; two cuts > 0.
- **Verdict.** n ≥ 20 qualifying days and ρ ≥ 0.50: **holds**. n ≥ 20 and
  ρ < 0.50: **fails**. n < 20: **undecided**, reported as such; that would be
  a finding about NESO's publication lag or the volume check. Ties get
  average ranks.
- **Why 0.50 and 20.** 0.50 means a clear monotone relation, not just a
  detectable one. With 20 days and no real relation, ρ ≥ 0.50 comes up about
  1.2 % of the time (one-tailed, t-approximation; the 5 % critical value is
  about 0.38). The window holds 197 settlement days before the falsifier
  date, so 20 is reachable even if NESO lags by weeks or the volume check fails
  on many of them.
- **Shown beside the verdict, never deciding.** ρ using the £ wind-bid share;
  ρ on days whose sign check holds; the scatter.
- **Cost.** A pure function over `tracker.json` rows, plus tests. It can be
  frozen well before 2026-09-26.

## Option B: split gas offers at the source with NESO's SO-flag

**T4-B.** On every qualifying day, NESO's Constraints figure is **at least**
the *SO-flagged cut*: the money on SO-flagged acceptances, gas-unit offers
plus wind-unit bids. It holds while every day holds and fails on the first
counterexample, like T2. T2 compared NESO against *all* wind bids and gas
offers; T4-B compares it only against the actions NESO itself marked as
constraint-affected. It asks whether NESO's constraint category contains its
own flagged actions, rather than guessing from the wind.

- **Which flag.** Only the **SO-flag**. Per Elexon's Imbalance Pricing
  Guidance (v15.0, 25 June 2020, §"System Operator Flagging"), "for BOAs, the
  SO flags when it believes the BOA may be impacted by a transmission
  constraint". The **CADL flag is not a constraint marker**. Elexon's systems
  set it on an acceptance series shorter than the Continuous Acceptance
  Duration Limit (10 minutes since 1 April 2019), to catch short-duration
  balancing. CADL counts would be reported beside the result and never enter
  the cut.
- **Data (new acquisition).** Elexon's settlement stack, endpoint
  `/balancing/settlement/stack/all/{bid|offer}/{date}/{period}` (dataset
  ISPSTACK). Per acceptance it has `id` (BM unit), `acceptanceId`, `soFlag`,
  `cadlFlag`, `volume`, `originalPrice`, `finalPrice`, `repricedIndicator`.
  The field names are verified in Elexon's published API spec on 2026-09-19;
  the spec gives no semantics, and the semantics above are Elexon's
  guidance. That is 96 calls per day, the same shape as DISPTAV.
- **Accounting layer.** The flagged £ would be volume × original price per
  acceptance: the pricing stack's view, not EBOCF cashflow. It is a fifth
  layer, never reconciled into L2. A containment rule needs a tolerance,
  and the tolerance depends on how far stack money and EBOCF money differ for
  the same unit and period. Measuring that is a calibration, not a schema pass
  (a schema pass computes no figure). It must be declared and run on days
  outside every window, before T4-B's threshold is written.
- **What must happen before it can be frozen.**
  - A schema pass on stack data for a day outside every window (for example
    2026-08-31), producing `archives/elexon-settlement-stack/` and computing no
    figure.
  - The declared calibration above, run on out-of-window days (August).
  - The runner's acquire phase extended, with tests, and deployed to
    `tracker-013`.
  - The declaration citing the schema report's digest.

  That is too much to do well before 2026-09-26. If it isn't done by then,
  T4-B's window starts at batch 3 (freeze before 2026-10-03), at no cost to
  its integrity.

## Recommendation

Freeze **A** before 2026-09-26. It tests the pattern the note promises to
test, with data the tracker already has. Prepare **B** properly and freeze
it for batch 3 onward: it is the better question (why NESO's figure
diverges, not just when), but it needs new data, a schema pass and a
deploy. The two do not conflict and can run side by side.
