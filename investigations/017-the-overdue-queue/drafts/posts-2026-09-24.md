# A. The queue fee (recommended)

One project on Britain's grid queue has appeared in 511 copies of NESO's TEC Register since 08/11/2018. Its status has read "Awaiting Consents" in every one. On 05/01/2024 the register gave its old name, Eggborough, to a different project, project id and all, so anyone tracking it by name has followed the wrong row.

This matters now. Ofgem is minded to approve CMP470, which would make battery projects in the queue secure at least £3,000 per MW against leaving it, stepping up every six months to £25,000. It is returned when a project energises; if the project leaves instead, that part of its cancellation charge goes to consumers. Ofgem counts about 90 GW of batteries operating or in Gate 2 against about 29 GW required. Responses close on 01/10/2026.

Security against leaving assumes the queue can tell who is still in it. In the register NESO published on 15/09/2026, 98 entries have a connection date already behind them and a status other than Built. 43 of the 98 print their plant type as "Energy Storage System" and nothing else, more than any other type on the list. Late, or connected and never updated? The register cannot say. It never dates its status column, and NESO overwrites every copy.

We are not saying any of these projects would forfeit anything. Our counting rules were sealed and timestamped first.

Source: Ofgem, CMP470 minded-to consultation, 17/09/2026.

# B. The confirmed tier

One project on Britain's grid queue has appeared in 511 copies of NESO's TEC Register since 08/11/2018. Its status has read "Awaiting Consents" in every one. The register never dates a status, so a reader of one copy cannot tell a value written last week from one written in 2018.

That matters more since the reform. NESO now puts projects it judges ready into Gate 2, the tier that carries a confirmed connection date. In the copy published on 15/09/2026, 17 of the 95 Gate 2 entries were already past that date: 17.9% of the tier's entries and 18.5% of its capacity. On a second reading two of the 17 may not be past at all (one prints its date with day and month either way round, and an earlier copy gave the other a later date), which leaves 15 of 95.

In the first of our copies that shows each of them in Gate 2, every one of the 17 dates had already passed. Our copies with a Gate column begin on 19/05/2026 and skip 95 days to 22/08/2026, so some dates may have passed inside that gap.

Stale or late? Only the old copies can tell, and NESO overwrites them. The rules were sealed before we counted.
