# Forward Mysteries — how candidates are generated and killed

**Declared 2026-08-23, before any F002 candidate was generated.** Frozen;
amendments go in the study that forces them. Written first for the same
reason `STAGE-TEST.md` was: a generator written *after* seeing candidates
can be gerrymandered to justify a favourite.

## What F001 established

F001's thesis died of **capture failure** — the predicted problem was
real, and an existing profession had already absorbed it, three years
before the study was declared. The corrected sequence:

> structural change → necessary function → **absorption kill** →
> externalisation pressure → capture

The failure was not bad luck. It was **predictable from the candidate's
shape**, and nothing in the process was looking for it. This protocol
exists to look for it first.

## Candidate generation: bias toward failed absorption

Candidates are **not** generated by asking "what sounds like an
interesting future bottleneck?" — that selects for narrative appeal.
They are generated by scanning for functions where **at least two or
three externalisation forces are already plausibly present**, because
those are the functions existing jobs should struggle to absorb.

The ten forces (from `CLAUDE.md`): volume beyond bespoke human scaling;
frequency shifting from per-transaction to continuous;
cross-organisational coordination no incumbent controls; independence
the performing party cannot self-supply; comparability across many
assets; data intensity exceeding judgement; liability someone must stand
behind; latency intolerance; standardisation of repetitive work; adviser
cost outgrowing transaction value.

Combinations that most strongly predict externalisation, and which
candidate generation should actively seek:

- **continuous + cross-organisational + data-intensive**
- **high-volume + latency-sensitive + standardisable**
- **independence-required + liability-bearing + comparable across assets**
- **specialist human workflow + rapidly rising transaction volume +
  expensive adviser labour**

A candidate carrying none of these is **not investigated**, however
interesting its story.

## The kill order, binding

1. **Establish the structural change only enough to justify looking.**
   Not exhaustively. F001 spent rounds of causal reasoning it did not
   need.
2. **Run the absorption kill.** One question, asked before any
   sophisticated argument: **"Who already gets paid to perform this
   function, and how?"** Not "does a startup exist"; not "is there
   software". Search for dated mandates, disclosed scopes of work,
   procurement notices, job specifications, budget lines.
3. **If the function is absorbed, the default conclusion is: no new
   category predicted.** The study stops there and records it. It may
   continue *only* on evidence that absorption is itself under strain,
   which means demonstrating a specific force — e.g. *frequency required
   > frequency provided*, or *evidence complexity > economically
   scalable adviser capacity*.
4. **Only then** earn the deeper investigation: constraint, consequence,
   recognition, capture.

**A candidate that dies at step 2 is a success**, and is recorded with
the same care as one that survives. Cheap deaths are the point: three or
four rapid ones will teach more about where opportunity is *not* than a
single surviving thesis would.

## Research allocation

Between steps, the next action is chosen by leverage, never by
curiosity or by which claim is most uncertain:

> **Which unresolved proposition has the highest chance of changing the
> final capture verdict, per unit of research effort?**

F001's retrospective answer was *"is this already standard technical
DD?"*, and it would have gone straight to a dated press release. That is
the standard to beat.

## Scoring, and what is preserved

Every closed forward study records a vector, never a verdict:
`(need, timing, adoption, capture)`, with the failure mode named —
**need failure** (the problem did not materialise) or **capture
failure** (it did, and existing institutions absorbed it). Studies that
die at step 2 are scored the same way, so the record accumulates
evidence about *where absorption succeeds*, which is itself the map the
machine is learning.

## Publication posture

A negative study is **not** published as a standalone prediction piece.
F001 is intellectually strong and commercially negative; publishing it
alone would read as either false modesty or a failure. It is preserved
as methodological foundation, and becomes publishable only as part of a
broader piece once several studies exist and the process is visibly
repeatable rather than one elegant failure:

> *We built a machine to find future businesses. Its first idea died in
> twenty minutes. That was the point.*

That piece requires F002 and F003 to have run. It is not written before
then.

## The structure this may eventually force in Morpholog

Recorded as a candidate, not proposed:

```
Change → NecessaryFunction → ExistingJob
                               ├── Absorbs
                               └── FailsToAbsorbBecause(force)
                                     → ExternalisedFunction
                                         → CaptureMechanism
```

`FailsToAbsorbBecause(force)` is the interesting predicate, and the ten
forces belong there **only if repeated studies validate them**. One
study is not evidence that a taxonomy is right.
