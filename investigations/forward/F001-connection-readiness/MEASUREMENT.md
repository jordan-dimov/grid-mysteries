## What this refutes

The declaration's F-5 asked whether an adequate solution already exists.
The answer, for the **function**, is that it has been **bought,
institutionalised inside incumbent technical due diligence, and
delivered with ongoing monitoring since at least 2023**. The declared
falsifier in `STAGE-TEST.md` reads:

> refuted if evidence with t_public ≤ 2026-08-22 shows recurring buyer
> procurement of connection-quality assessment as a distinct line item —
> repeat mandates, standard DD scope inclusion, or a named budget

**"Standard DD scope inclusion" is satisfied.** ABL's disclosed Phase 1
scope names grid connections as an assessed item, and Phase 2 is
recurring monitoring with milestone certification. **The "category near
birth" classification is refuted for the function.**

It survives, narrowly and unimpressively, only for the *standalone*
category: one dated supplier six weeks old, with no evidence of a single
purchase.

## Conclusion

> **F001's thesis does not survive its own test.** The need it predicted
> is real, regulator-driven and increasing — and it was already being
> served, bought and institutionalised inside incumbent technical due
> diligence roughly three years before the study was declared. What is
> genuinely new is a thin standalone supply layer, six weeks old at the
> cutoff, with **no observable buyer**.

Under `STAGE-TEST.md`'s outcome rules this is the third case:
institutionalisation and incumbent absorption are established, so the
study **substantially rediscovered an established function** rather than
detecting a category near birth. The honest classification is **workflow
transition at most** — bespoke advisory work with the first specialist
and software entrants appearing — and **not** category emergence.

**No opportunity is demonstrated.** Layer 5 of the declaration required
three propositions earned separately; the third — existing solutions are
inadequate — is not merely unearned but contradicted for the function.

This is a successful study by the declaration's own terms. It killed a
superficially attractive thesis in one working session, using an
instrument frozen before the measurement, at the cost of one Companies
House lookup and one dated press release.

## What the study is entitled to keep

- **Candidate B is now evidenced, not speculative.** Necessity of a
  *function* does not imply necessity of a *category*. The economy
  absorbed this one into an existing job — technical due diligence —
  and the absorption predates the regulatory change that supposedly
  creates the need. Any future forward study must test absorption
  before predicting a market.
- The **three-clock discipline** worked and cost nothing here, because
  visibility lag turned out to be nil. Its value is untested until a
  case where the profiles diverge.
- The **question that remains open**, and is not answered by this
  measurement: whether incumbent technical DD assesses connection
  *durability over time* or a point-in-time position. That is a real
  unknown, but it is now a question about *the adequacy of an existing
  service*, not about an unserved need — a materially weaker
  proposition, and one the study is not permitted to inflate.

## The burden of proof on the one open question has inverted

Whether incumbent DD assesses connection *durability over time* or a
point-in-time position remains genuinely open. But after F-5 its
standard has changed completely:

- **before F-5** — maybe there is a new market;
- **after F-5** — show *why the incumbent service becomes inadequate
  under changing conditions*.

Concretely, resurrecting commercial interest would now require evidence
that **frequency required > frequency the incumbent workflow provides**,
or that **new evidence complexity > economically scalable adviser
capacity**. Without one of those, this is a feature somebody adds to
technical due diligence, not a category. That presumption is now the
default and the study is not permitted to argue around it.
