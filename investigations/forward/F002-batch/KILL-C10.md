## 1. Existing job

Producing grid-impact and consent evidence for a large new load in GB is
already a named, paid function, and the 2026 record shows exactly who does
it. The three data-centre section 35 requests published on gov.uk this
year were prepared by planning/infrastructure law firms (Burges Salmon LLP
for CSE52 Ltd, Dartford; TLT LLP for Questpit Ltd, Bedford) with need and
socio-economic evidence from Knight Frank, Volterra Partners LLP, Montagu
Evans and others; the Wapseys Wood request lists a conventional project
team (lead consultant/MEP designer, architect, civil engineer). Grid-side
evidence comes from the connection process itself: Dartford cites a
National Grid Electricity Transmission transitional offer for 300 MW and
an expected NESO Gate 2 firm offer "later this year"; Bedford has applied
to NESO for an initial 200 MW. The 2025 NSIP route is verified: HCWS966
(15 Oct 2025) laid the draft Infrastructure Planning (Business or
Commercial Projects) (Amendment) Regulations; made 8 Jan 2026; data centres
can now opt in via a section 35 direction, with thresholds deferred to a
DSIT National Policy Statement not yet consulted on. So: planning
consultancies, planning law firms, economics consultancies,
MEP/engineering firms, and the DNO/NESO offer process. The incumbent named
in the frozen row is correct.

## 2. Eligibility of the frozen instruments (before numbers)

- **MHCLG planning statistics**: data centres are *not* separately
  identifiable. The PS2 open-data schema (file 202603) has major types:
  dwellings; offices/R&D/light industry; general industry/storage/
  warehousing; retail and services; traveller pitches; all other; Technical
  Details Consent. Worse for detection: 77% of major decisions in Q1 2026
  were under a performance agreement/EoT/EIA, and duration bands exist only
  for the non-PA cohort — so lengthening determination times on exactly the
  cases that would carry a data centre are invisible by construction.
- **PINS appeal statistics**: no development-type breakdown. A data-centre
  appeal wave of a few dozen cases would be lost in ~9,500 s78 receipts a
  year.
- **Consultancy rate cards / vacancies**: no public rate card for planning
  or grid-connection consultancy was found (recruiter pages carry no
  planning figures; Indeed and Glassdoor block retrieval). RTPI State of the
  Profession gives salary bands, not rates, and no private-sector rate
  series.
- **Distinguishability**: even visible movement would be inseparable from
  the general major-application backlog, general fee inflation, and the
  parallel demand for the same grid specialists from generation/storage
  developers (which the one recruiter narrative names as the driver).

The predicted symptoms could appear only as a diffuse, unattributable
signal. **The instruments are largely ineligible for this candidate.**

## 3. The frozen forcing observation, run

**Determination times (MHCLG, Jan–Mar 2026 release, 19 Jun 2026)** [V]: 91%
of major applications decided within 13 weeks *or agreed time* (unchanged
y/y); 19% within the statutory 13 weeks (unchanged y/y). Applications
received: 79,600 in Q1 2026 (−10% y/y); 315,900 in the year to March 2026
(−5%). Release's own caveat: 77% of major decisions involved a performance
agreement; 94% of those "on time" versus 81% without.

**PS2 open data, own computation (England, FY Apr–Mar)** [V]: major general
industry/storage/warehousing decisions 987 (FY22/23) → 954 → 795 → 717
(FY25/26); PA/EoT share 50% (FY17/18) → 77% (FY25/26); among non-PA
decisions, share within 13 weeks ≈ 88–92% throughout. Major offices/R&D/
light industry 541 (FY17/18) → 289 (FY25/26); PA share 53% → 74%. The
measurable population is shrinking and the unmeasured population is
growing — a data-quality trend, not a determination-time trend.

**NSIP timelines** [V]: MHCLG's streamlining consultation (updated 3 Jul
2026): NSIPs took on average 3.6 years to consent in 2024 versus 2.6 in
2012; pre-application "almost doubled between 2013 and 2021". No
data-centre DCO has yet been examined. The s35 stage itself is fast:
Wapseys Wood request 21 Jan 2026 → direction 16 Mar 2026 (~8 weeks); Bedford
24 Apr → 15 Jun (~7 weeks); Dartford 1 May → 1 Jul (~9 weeks). The PINS
national-infrastructure register shows none of the three at
pre-application yet.

**Appeal volumes (PINS release, 23 Apr 2026)** [V]: s78 appeals received Apr
2025–Mar 2026: 9,476 vs 9,158 (+3.5%). Median decision time 21 weeks (all),
28 weeks inquiries; March 2026 alone 18 weeks. Open cases 11,874 (Apr 2025)
→ 9,796 (Mar 2026); Jan 2025 update: median 27 weeks, 12,915 open. Caveat:
provisional, "known anomalies". **Direction: improving, not lengthening.**

**Consultant rates / vacancies**: RTPI State of the Profession 2025,
England [V via reader proxy]: n=2,217; modal band £40–50k; 50.5% say their
team lacks capacity frequently/always (66.2% public vs 31% private); LPA
survey 96.9% report difficulty recruiting at principal level; *no*
authority reported difficulty recruiting energy/renewables specialists.
No day rates, no rate trend. Allen & York grid-connection page [S,
undated]: analyst £45–65k … head of grid access £130–160k+; "severe market
imbalance" attributed to wind, solar and IPP developers "targeting the
exact same pool". Glassdoor snippet (Jun 2026) [S]: 408 "grid specialist"
jobs UK. **No source gives a consultancy day rate, rate card, or rate time
series.**

## 4. Contradicting evidence

- PINS throughput and open caseload improving; median decision time fell
  27 → 21 weeks in fifteen months.
- MHCLG's measurable cohort shows no lengthening; counts are falling.
- Government is standardising the route: opt-in NSIP status, three s35
  directions inside six months, a data-centre biodiversity gain statement
  (16 Jun 2026), an NPS in preparation, and a consultation to cut NSIP
  pre-application from ~2 years to 12 months.
- The applicants' own documents show the existing adviser ecosystem
  absorbing the new route immediately, using recovered-appeal precedents
  (Court Lane Dec 2024, Woodlands Park Jul 2025) as template evidence.
- Wapseys Wood sidesteps the grid-evidence problem with a 270–350 MW
  on-site gas energy centre — a substitution that shrinks the burden rather
  than externalising it.

## 5. Verdict

**Plausible but not measured.** The frozen observation cannot see the
symptom. Where the instruments *do* measure, they show no movement (MHCLG)
or improvement (PINS). The candidate is not killed outright — a 720 MW
campus demonstrably needs bespoke consent and grid evidence, and NSIP
consenting is slowing in aggregate — but nothing observed converts "the
work exists and is growing" into "the existing job is failing to absorb
it". Providers exist and are visibly being paid (Burges Salmon, TLT,
Volterra, Knight Frank); **that is absorption working**, and it must not be
promoted into buyer pain. Under v3, downgraded.

Demand side (not a substitute for the frozen observation): three s35
directions granted Mar–Jul 2026 for campuses of c.300 MW, 720 MW and
300 MW; Dartford holds an NGET transitional offer for 300 MW with a Gate 2
firm offer expected in 2026; Bedford has a 200 MW NESO application;
permitted comparators cited by applicants include Humber Tech Park 384 MW
(Aug 2024) and Equinix DC10 320 MW (Jan 2025). A handful of projects at
direction/offer stage, none yet at DCO pre-application, one pivoting to
on-site gas. Proceeding to *construction* is not evidenced in what was
retrieved.

## 6. What this says about the generator

The generator paired a specialist-human-workflow candidate with
instruments structurally blind to it. Two lessons: (i) a cheap forcing
observation must be checked for *type-identifiability* before being frozen
— MHCLG and PINS aggregate away every project type this candidate cares
about, and a rising share of agreed-time decisions actively removes the
measurable population; (ii) "consultancy rate cards and vacancies" is not
a public instrument in GB professional services — no rate series exists to
move — so any candidate whose symptom is priced adviser labour needs a
proxy declared in advance (LPA PPA charge schedules, PINS cost-recovery
fees, company-level fee income) or it will always return "not measured".
Separately, this run repeats the F001 pattern: a newly formalised route
(opt-in NSIP) was absorbed within weeks by the same law and consultancy
firms, and "existing job first" would have predicted that before any
statistics were pulled.
