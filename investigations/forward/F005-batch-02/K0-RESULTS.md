K0 asks one thing: does the function have an identifiable economic owner
today? Class **A** external spend · **B** internal spend by an identifiable
budget owner · **C** dated hard obligation on an identifiable budget owner.
Growth of the forcing variable and the existence of providers are not
evidence.

**Retrieval limitation, stated up front.** The session's web-search budget
was exhausted before K0 began; every item came from direct fetches of
publisher URLs. Every DNO website, ENA and Regen returned 403/404 to both
tools, and Contracts Finder / Find a Tender keyword search is unusable
without a session. Consequently **class A (a named buyer paying a named
third party under a dated notice) was not established for any candidate
by a procurement record**; where A is recorded it rests on a regulator's
fee schedule (C16) or on a DNO's dated description of the market structure
plus dated contractor payroll (C17). This biases K0 toward classes B and
C. It does not change any verdict — one class suffices — but it is why
the class distribution below should not be read as a finding about how
these functions are bought.

## Source caveats carried forward (not K0 failures)

- **C11**: the obligation is per quotation, not "at volume"; connect-and-notify installations generate no quotation. LVSVD funds LV services delivery; quotation labour sits inside it by inference.
- **C12**: DCC binds transmission-connected demand only; the Distribution Code sets limits to meet, not an explicit evidence-package duty; NESO's published process is generator-framed and only "best practice" for demand. That asymmetry is K1/K2 material.
- **C14**: NESO evidence is transmission-demand only; the IRN obliges customers to supply data, not NESO to forecast conversion; Ofgem's own phrase "far exceeding even the most ambitious forecasts" concedes the incumbent forecast failed — K1/K2 material.
- **C15**: the direction covers generation and demand alike and mandates the accounting definition, not telemetry or settlement systems; transmission-level non-firm demand not covered.
- **C16**: the *specified generator* route largely exempts emergency-only sets (≤50 h testing); the *MCP* route (≥1 MWth, no hours exemption) is what bites. Fees are regulator fees; consultancy spend not directly evidenced.
- **C17**: no individual buyer→ICP contract notice retrieved; NERS fee schedule not retrieved.
- **C18**: the central register is not live; today's registration is per system operator under the rules; licence-condition number and BSC modification ID not verified.
- **C19**: a data-centre-named HV AP job specification was not found (Microsoft's London "Shift Critical Environment Technician", 20 Aug 2026, mentions generators but no HV authorisation); the data-centre sub-segment is inferred from private-network contractors. HTM 06-03 is guidance ("should"), not statute.

## One observation, recorded and not acted on

Every one of the eight passes is carried wholly or partly by **class C**
— a dated obligation on a regulated licensee or a statutory permit holder.
In a sector as regulated as GB electricity, almost any forced function
will have an obligated owner, so K0's discriminating power here may be
low: it is built to catch the Bulgarian failure (F004: no owner at all),
and that failure may be rare inside GB regulation and common outside it.
This is consistent with L14 ("the buyer term is market-specific") and is
logged as an observation for the cross-batch record. It is **not** a
reason to raise the K0 bar now; the batch's scoring was pre-declared and
the next question — whether obligated owners absorb the function
adequately — is exactly what K1/K2 exist to measure.
