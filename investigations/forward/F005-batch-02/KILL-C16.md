## 1. Current absorber

**Regulator side (England).** The EA issues MCP/SG permits in three tiers
— standard rules (low risk), simple bespoke (low risk, no dispersion
modelling), complex bespoke (high risk, site-specific modelling).
Standard rules permits are only usable for Tranche-B-only SG sites; NRW no
longer issues standard rules at all. Applications go through a "duly
made" gate. Source: gov.uk *Specified generator: apply for an
environmental permit* (first pub 2019-07-15, updated 2026-02-26). [V] The
corporate scorecard's own taxonomy puts standard rules permits in
"category 3" (140-day target) and bespoke assessments "involving wide
consultation, pollution modelling and assessment, the utilisation of
specialist skills" in "category 4" (250-day target). [V] The "National
Permitting Service" named in the frozen row is not found in any fetched
source. [N]

**Rule sets.** SR2018 No 1–9 were created via Standard rules consultation
No 17 (2018). As of the 2026-01-19 decision: SR2018 No 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9
withdrawn for new applicants from 28 Feb 2026; SR2018 No 7 and SR2022 No 9
remain and gain hydrogen and gas-oil substitutes. [V]

**Operator side.** The EA's 2018 design consultation asked operators what
job roles their staff responsible for applications and compliance support
hold, and whether they expect third-party costs "such as having to hire
consultants". [V]

**Consultancies.** EA guidance: for SCAIL screening "you should ask an air
quality consultant to complete this for you"; for detailed modelling "You
should find a consultant to do it for you." [V] The 2026 SR consultation
drew 1 consultancy among 10 respondents. [V]

**Compliance reporting.** Assessed through NCAD assessment types and
non-compliances recorded via CCS categories 1–4, which also adjust
subsistence charges ("Less compliant sites will pay a surcharge …
operators with perfect compliance records can receive a discount"). [V]

## 2. Absorption status

**ADEQUATE** — *expanded successfully (simplification and cost recovery)*
and *forcing variable not yet arrived*, plus a policy option that could
abolish the function.

- **Force-side volume is small and not growing in the observable
  register.** EPR Industrial Sites register (.accdb dated 2026-04-01):
  distinct permits with any MCP/SG activity **393** (370 effective). New
  MCP/SG permits by effective year: 2019 45, 2020 33, 2021 32, 2022 21,
  2023 14, 2024 26, 2025 8. [V] *Gap:* this register covers process-
  industry sites; standalone standard-rules MCP/SG permits appear largely
  absent (393 permits vs the scorecard's ~4,500/yr category-3 applications
  across all regimes). No MCP/SG-specific register exists on data.gov.uk.
  The true standalone SG permit population is **unmeasured here**.
- **Uptake of the bespoke SG standard rules collapsed, and the EA
  simplified.** EA, 2026-01-19: "there have been no applications made for
  these rules sets [SR2018 No 2, 3] since 2023"; "one operator currently
  holds a permit for SR2018 No 5 and 2 operators hold permits for SR2018
  No 9. There are no permit holders for the other rules sets." Respondent
  explanation: "most new SGs are over 1 megawatts thermal … used for grid
  export, while plants under 1MWth typically operate in island mode and
  are exempt". [V]
- **Charging capacity was expanded.** EA cost-of-service consultation
  (Apr–May 2025; response 2025-08-01; new charges 1 Aug 2025): schemes
  "last reviewed in 2018"; cumulative CPI ~26%; the increase "will allow us
  to maintain the service we provide … invest in digital transformation";
  further 3.8% uplift 1 Apr 2026. Respondents' complaint: "The service
  provided by the EA must be adequate and provide licences and permits
  within an adequate timescale." [V]
- **The forcing variable has not arrived.** MCP guidance (2025-07-09):
  existing MCP 1–5 MWth "permit … by 1 January 2029 … you cannot apply
  yet", comply by 1 Jan 2030. SG guidance: Tranche A 1–5 MWth and <1 MWth
  dates 1 Jan 2030. Emergency-only backup generators are excluded from SG
  rules but "[are] a MCP and require a permit by the appropriate
  deadline". [V] The large-load standby fleet's permitting wave is a 2029
  event.
- **Absorption may be redesigned out of existence.** EA (2026-01-19):
  "a registration-based approach (as proposed for MCPs from 2029 in a
  recent … Defra EPR regulation reform consultation) … We are awaiting
  Defra's consultation response about registration as an alternative to
  permitting for MCP and SG." [V as EA statement; Defra consultation not
  fetched — S] Scorecard 2025-26 Q4: "the removal of some activities from
  permitting requirements". [V]
