## The answer to the question

> When a supplier's customers hold batteries that respond to the
> supplier's own prices, whose problem is the resulting position — and
> is that job being done?

**On a § 41a dynamic tariff, the batteries do not respond to the
supplier's price.** Every pinned German tariff — five Stadtwerke,
1KOMMA5°, Octopus Germany — passes the EPEX day-ahead (SDAC) price
through "1:1"; that price is fixed at the auction before the
balancing-group party nominates its schedule. The supplier holds no
price position on the segment's energy. What it holds is an
**imbalance position**: the difference between the fleet's forecast
response to a known price and its actual response, settled
quarter-hourly under the balancing-group contract. **Whose problem**: the
balancing-group responsible party — for most Stadtwerke a portfolio
manager (Trianel, Syneco, EnBW's Energy Factory, Vattenfall Energy
Trading, each selling "Bilanzkreismanagement" with a day-ahead forecast
team), for a large Stadtwerk its own portfolio-management risk committee
(Bochum). **Is the job done**: yes, as ordinary balancing-group work; no
portfolio manager names dynamic-tariff load, and no supplier, association
or dispute body is shown complaining that it cannot be forecast.

**The reflexive book proper** — a supplier that sets its own price *and*
moves the devices against it — exists, and is held in-house by the
specialists: Intelligent Octopus Germany (a single fixed price, batteries
controlled from launch, 6 May 2025), Tibber Smart Battery ("lädt … wenn
die Preise klein sind", Grid Rewards in 2026), 1KOMMA5° Heartbeat AI
("pauschale Softwaregebühr" instead of a per-kWh margin), Enpal.One
(25 March 2024, marketing the surplus), and in GB Octopus under Kraken,
whose licensees (E.ON Next, EDF, Origin before October 2023; Tokyo Gas
October 2023; Plenitude, National Grid US by September 2025) buy
"Residential Flex … devices responding automatically to prices and
system conditions" as part of a £334 m recurring licence base. That book
is closed by control, not by forecasting, and it never belonged to the
Stadtwerke.

**In Great Britain**, from M15 (7 May 2027) every supplier's imbalance is
settled on what each meter point actually did; the position is the
supplier's own or its outsourced trading function's under the BSC, as
today for half-hourly customers. The price-and-export arbitrage 010
computed exists only where a supplier writes both prices — Octopus, on
Kraken. Smart time-of-use tariffs were 2.8 % of the domestic market in
July 2025.

## The three propositions

1. **The problem is coming** — earned, dated: § 41a since 1 January 2025
   for every supplier; § 45 quotas with 77 enforcement proceedings from
   27 March 2026; 2.72 million battery units under 30 kW in operation;
   MHHS M14 28 October 2026, M15 7 May 2027.
2. **A buyer is compelled** — earned for imbalance management (the
   balancing-group contract; BSC settlement on actuals) and for interval
   billing (§ 41a(7); M14's loss of new customers); **not** for reflexive
   forecasting as generated, because the price is not the supplier's;
   **not** for pairing rules outside the specialists; **not** for
   inverter-versus-meter reconciliation.
3. **Existing solutions are inadequate** — **not earned anywhere.**
   Balancing-group management is a mature sold service; billing is a
   vendor release; the strained link (metering) is a formed category with
   the specialists installing their own meters.

**Honest output: a described problem. No opportunity is named.** The
sponsor's first falsifier (absorbed) fired for the imbalance book; the
question's premise — load moving against the supplier's *own* price —
held only for the specialists, who are the absorbers, not the buyers.

  R2 (imbalance) and, by construction, for the control book at the

  the Stadtwerke tariffs are genuine 1:1 pass-throughs with a visible
  fixed layer and no export product; whether households take them is
  the same unmeasured term. Watch: 2026-11.

## Lessons for the generator (logged, not promoted)

- **L20 (proposed).** "Reflexive" is a property of who sets the price.
  Before generating functions from a load that "responds to the
  supplier's price", ask **who fixes the price the load responds to, and
  is it known before the position must be nominated?** A pass-through of
  a day-ahead auction is not reflexive to the supplier; a supplier-set
  price with device control is, and there the loop is closed by control.
  F007 generated three functions (R1, R3, R4) on the wrong answer.
- **L21 (proposed).** A function generated from one market's product
  boundary must be re-derived in the other market before it is frozen:
  010's pairing rules exist because Octopus sells both import and export;
  a German household's export is the state feed-in tariff. The generator
  should name the **counterparty on each side of the household meter**
  per market.
- **L22 (proposed).** Documentary instruments (an incumbent's marketing
  page, a rule text) produce weak nulls. The gate should grade
  "eligible now — documentary" below "eligible now — numeric" and the
  funnel should report them separately, which this study does by hand.
