# Phantom liquidity: what GB Balancing Mechanism data is entitled to tell you

*Method Study 001 research note. All numbers are reproducible from the
pinned corpus (2026-08-04..2026-08-10, 336 settlement periods); see
`METHOD-STUDY-001.md` for the pre-declared metrics and
`evidence/analysis.json` for every figure quoted here.*

## Candidate headline

> **A naive screen of one week of public GB balancing data found 3,856,031
> cases where the system operator appeared to ignore a better-priced
> action. Two-thirds of them involved an alternative that provably could
> not deliver a single megawatt.**

And the honest second half, stated with equal prominence:

> The remaining third — 1.26 million pairwise cases, from 82,664 distinct
> unit-period alternatives — **survives** the conservative public
> feasibility checks applied here. Surviving a feasibility check is not
> evidence of operator error; it is a measure of how much apparent
> liquidity public data alone cannot adjudicate.

## The numbers, in the only form that is honest

Raw pairwise counting is exposure-weighted by construction: one standing,
undeliverable submission can generate thousands of pairwise "inversions"
against every accepted action of the week. So the result must always be
read as a combination:

- **67.3%** of the 3,856,031 raw pairwise inversions (2,595,381) were
  generated by alternatives that were **provably non-deliverable** from
  public physical state — the unit had zero headroom in the relevant
  direction at every instant of the settlement period (FPN against
  MILS/MELS endpoint extremes, with registered-capacity fallbacks).
- Those pairwise cases collapse to **76,289 of 158,953 unique
  alternatives (48.0%)**.
- Concentration is real but not total: the top **100** of 63,009
  (unit, submitted-price) groups generate **24.6%** of all raw pairwise
  inversions; the single largest standing submission alone generates
  0.7%.
- Persistence is bimodal: the **median** alternative appears in just
  **1 of 336** settlement periods, but inversion counts are dominated by
  standing submissions — the inversion-weighted mean persistence is
  **32.1%**, and the top decile of alternatives is submitted
  more than half of the week.
- The registered hypothesis (`hyp-ms-001-majority-non-deliverable`: more
  than half of raw pairwise inversions involve a clearly non-deliverable
  alternative) is **confirmed** at 67.3%.

## What the residual is and is not

The 82,664 surviving alternatives are *not* a queue of operator errors.
They are the set that public physical state alone cannot dismiss. Their
price gaps are mostly modest (median best-gap £37.36/MWh, 90th percentile
£145.00, maximum £3,000.00), they sit in 561 of 564 episodes, and the
largest contributors are persistent, moderately-priced offer-side
submissions from wind units — a shape consistent with locational
curtailment economics, which this study deliberately did not test.
Intra-period timing, ramp rates, stable limits, prior instructions and
constraint location all remain open. Under this project's protocol those
mechanisms are *compatible but unproven* explanations for any individual
residual case until tested.

## Why this matters

If you treat submitted bid-offer ladders as available flexibility — for a
backtest, a "curtailment cost" estimate, a battery revenue model, or a
market-inefficiency claim — this corpus says roughly **two-thirds of your
apparent counterfactuals never existed physically**, and the feasible
remainder is still untested against timing, dynamics and location. The
hard problem in public energy data is not acquiring it; it is knowing what
the data is entitled to tell you.
