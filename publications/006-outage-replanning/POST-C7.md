To upgrade the grid, you have to switch parts of it off.

Finding safe windows to do it is a big job, and getting bigger.

NESO puts around **2,500 outages** into the year-ahead plan for the
transmission network.

It then processes **more than 17,000 changes** to that plan during the
year.

Every change means the detailed assessment starts again. A team of **65
engineers** does this.

National Grid's latest numbers show the same pressure from the other
side. Against the previous year, new outage requests raised *during* the
year rose:

**+51%** before the optimisation stage
**+35%** during optimisation
**+52%** in the final delivery stage — 1,438 to 2,184

And here is the part I keep thinking about.

The year-ahead plan itself got **better**. National Grid delivered 50% of
it, up from 46%, and says this reverses a three-year decline.

So the plan is improving — while new requests raised against it are
growing sharply.

One more detail. One of National Grid's own KPIs is called "Outage
coordination". It still cannot be reported, because of how the
information is recorded in eNAMS, the shared outage system.

Nobody is ignoring this. NESO's System Access Reform is under way, and
National Grid says it is running planning and delivery transformation
programmes into the next price control.

It's a race, not a breakdown.

National Grid names the broad causes: network security, asset faults,
customer requests.

What I can't see from outside is where the effort actually lands.

So if you work in network access planning or outage delivery —
**of those thousands of changes, which ones eat most of the engineering
time?**

*Sources: NESO Network Access Planning and System Access Reform; National
Grid Network Access Planning KPIs, July 2026.*
